\section{Main Spatial Strategies}
The spatial organization of the \textit{Connect it} project is driven by two primary strategies that reconcile technical infrastructure with urban quality. The first focuses on the structural logic of a "nomadic" bridge system, while the second addresses the integration of public space and community placemaking.

\subsection{Strategy 1: Modular Structural Concept}
The structural concept is defined by the structure being living infrastructure that follows the city’s transformation. In response to the long-term disruption of the KnoopXL redevelopment, the bridge is designed to be disassembled, moved, and reconfigured across different construction phases, thus it adapts to changing conditions on the site. This ensures that the investment in connectivity is not lost when a specific site phase concludes, but is instead relocated to where it is needed most. This has potential to be used after the redevelopment is done in other parts of Eindhoven, or even other cities.

Initially inspired by the utilitarian efficiency of urban scaffolding, the concept evolved into a structural landmark. While it retains the prefabrication logic of temporary systems, the bridge's triangular truss offers it structural integrity, modularity and presence, as such structures are not always chosen due to their cost. This aesthetic shift ensures the bridge functions as a recognizable destination and a symbol of innovation for Eindhoven, rather than a mere bypass.

The system is discretized into a "kit-of-parts" using standard-sized components. To facilitate transport and assembly, the primary 30m spans are subdivided into modules of 15m, 7m, or 9m. While the current configuration is optimized for the Kruisstraat site, this modular logic allows for significant future flexibility. The topology and cross-sections of the members were developed using a parametric workflow, allowing for rapid iteration to achieve an interesting and high-performance structural form that remains efficient to fabricate.

The concept of circularity is embedded in the structural joints. By utilizing bolted connections between major modules, the bridge can be "split" at the end of its lifecycle. This allows the system to be repurposed into two or three smaller bridges for use in other urban contexts, such as the TU/e campus or the clusters 4 and 5 of Knoop-XL, ensuring long-term value beyond the initial Knoop-XL timeline.
